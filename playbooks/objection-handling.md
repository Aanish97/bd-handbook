# Objection Handling

Objections are signals of interest, not rejection. The method is the same every
time; the content is specific to GrowthCode.

## The method: Acknowledge → Ask → Respond → Confirm

1. **Acknowledge** — "That's a fair concern." Never argue.
2. **Ask** — dig into what's really behind it. The first objection is rarely the
   real one.
3. **Respond** — address the actual concern with proof, not a monologue.
4. **Confirm** — "Does that put the concern to rest?" Then move forward.

## Common objections

> **Fill in:** Replace the responses with our real, proof-backed answers. Keep
> them honest — a response that over-promises creates a worse problem later.

### "It's too expensive."

* **Really means:** unclear ROI, or comparing to the wrong thing.
* **Respond:** reframe to value and cost-of-inaction. _Fill in our ROI framing._

### "We already use {{competitor / in-house}}."

* **Really means:** switching cost fear, or genuine satisfaction.
* **Respond:** contrast on the one thing we do materially better; don't trash
  them. _Fill in._

### "Now's not a good time."

* **Really means:** no urgency / no compelling event.
* **Respond:** tie to a cost of waiting or an upcoming trigger. _Fill in._

### "Send me some info."

* **Really means:** polite brush-off, or a real gatekeeper step.
* **Respond:** agree to send *and* book the next step to discuss it. Never let it
  end the conversation.

### "I need to talk to my team / boss."

* **Really means:** you may not be talking to the economic buyer.
* **Respond:** offer to join that conversation; ask what *they'd* need to see.

## When to walk away

Not every deal is winnable. If there's no pain, no budget, and no path to the
decision-maker after honest effort — disqualify and spend the time on a real
deal. Record the lost reason.
